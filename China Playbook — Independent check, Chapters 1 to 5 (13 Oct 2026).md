# China Playbook — Independent check, Chapters 1 to 5 (13 Oct 2026)

Independent fact-check of "New draft, Chapters 1 to 5 (12 Oct 2026)" against free, public sources. Prepared 13 Oct 2026. I did not write the draft; the task was to find errors, not to improve the prose.

**Inputs used:** the five chapters and their sources files (file `1-5`), "Round 4 review, verdicts and draft fixes (13 Oct 2026)" and "Trade data 2025 update and price-per-kg test (13 Oct 2026)". The two outline files in the repo (`.docx`, `.pptx`) were not part of the brief and were not checked.

**Headline:** 140 claims checked (a row may cover a few adjacent numbers): **41 CONFIRMED, 72 MINOR FIX, 7 WRONG, 20 UNSOURCED.**

The core trade numbers are sound: the 28-market screen (26 of 28 up, 14 at 50%+), Indonesian excavators, Indian cranes, Malaysian generator sets, moulding machines and the laser and moulding duty effects all reproduce from public UN Comtrade data. The errors cluster in four places: (1) causal or comparative sentences that go beyond the evidence (Japan "most of China's gain", "credit moves buyers", "price gap was small"); (2) quotations and attributions that do not match the source (Cummins, Mandiri Tunas, Haitian, Press Note 2, two Chapter 2 statements by a United Tractors executive); (3) a few mislabelled or non-reproducible statistics (Germany in generator sets and lasers, Thai forklift definitions, every Vietnam-based figure); and (4) company-only anchors, above all Komatsu's 29% → 20% share, which is United Tractors' own estimate. The seven WRONG rows are 1.09, 1.35, 1.39, 2.18, 2.19, 3.07 and 3.17. The ten most important fixes are at the end.

## How this check was done, and its limits

**Verdicts**

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CONFIRMED | The source says what the draft says: same figure, year, scope and attribution. |
| MINOR FIX | Right in substance, but wording, rounding, scope, date or attribution needs correcting. |
| WRONG | The source (or a reproduction from public data) contradicts the draft. |
| UNSOURCED | No public source found that supports it, or the cited source does not contain it. |

**Method.** Every number, date, name and factual claim was traced to the source in the sources file where one is given, otherwise to a free public source found by search. Where a page loaded, I read the passage itself. Where a page refused automated access (HTTP 403/503), I say so in the source column and either found the same text elsewhere or marked the row as checked by search snippet only. I did not invent any figure. Where I could not verify something, the row says so.

**Trade data was reproduced, not trusted.** The sources file points to internal "Archetype dossiers" for most trade claims, which a reader cannot open. I pulled the same series myself from the free UN Comtrade public API (`https://comtradeapi.un.org/public/v1/preview/C/A/HS`, imports, HS 6-digit, `partner2Code=0`, `motCode=0`, `customsCode=C00`) for India (699), Indonesia (360), Thailand (764) and Malaysia (458). Results are in Appendix A. Limits: (a) Vietnam (704) returns no rows from the free API, so every draft figure that includes Vietnam cannot be reproduced; (b) the draft does not give HS codes for CNC lathes, machining centres or lasers, so I used HS 845811, 845710 and 845611; (c) "established makers" uses the Round 4 country list.

**Sources I could not open** (automated access refused): Bisnis.com, Mining.com, RentalYard, The Paper, Yieh, katalog.inaproc.id (e-Katalog), IDX (Hexindo expose PDF), RRI, Agg-Net, Equipment India (some pages), Equipment Indonesia (XCMG–GMT page), IDNFinancials, NNA. These rows are tagged `[not opened]` or `[snippet only]`.

## The 10 most important fixes

1. **Chapter 3: "India's crane market gives the clearest proof that credit moves buyers" (3.17).** The credit the regulator found was a Chinese exporter lending to its own Indian importer. The findings are about dumping, not about why buyers chose. Replace with the 3.17 wording, and stop using the case as proof in Chapters 3 and 8.
2. **Chapter 1: "Most of China's gain came out of Japan's share" (1.09).** Public Comtrade data show Japan's fall equals 35–40% of China's gain in excavators, cranes and forklifts. Say Japan lost share in every product, but that explains well under half of China's gain in the largest categories.
3. **Price: "The price gap was small", "A small price gap did the rest", "price played only a small part" (1.33, 1.34, 2.05, 2.16, X2).** The 7% catalogue comparison is against Komatsu's own discount model and cannot be opened; SANY was reported 20% below Japanese makers; Komatsu cut price 10–15% in response; established excavators cost 1.9× per kg in Indonesia. Decide the story and reconcile all three chapters.
4. **Chapter 1 lender and quality paragraph (1.35–1.39).** Adira's financing doubled for Chinese and non-Chinese brands alike; Mandiri Tunas's quote is "reasonably affordable", and the same director says non-Chinese quality is more assured and blames the weak rupiah, which Adira and BFI deny. Delete "Quality has caught up" and "Exchange rates do little".
5. **Chapter 2: "demand ran at more than twice supply" and "moved Komatsu output from Japan and India to Indonesia" (2.18, 2.19).** Both misstate the UT executive's remarks. Use the 2.18 and 2.19 wording.
6. **Chapter 3: the Cummins quotation (3.07, 3.08).** The words in quotation marks are not in the call transcript; Southeast Asia is not tied to data-centre backup power. Quote the transcript.
7. **SANY branch counts and unsourced dealer facts (2.28, 4.08, 4.09, 4.22).** Three branch counts (26, 24, 44) conflict; XCMG's "15-minute, 24-hour, 72-hour" policy and GM Tractors' "14 branches" have no public source. Use "24 branches and 55 points, July 2025" and cut the unsourced claims.
8. **Chapter 5: Press Note 2 (5.19).** The 60-day route, the capital-goods scope and the majority-ownership condition are not in the Press Note. Cite the SOP or FEMA rules, or reword as in 5.19.
9. **Trade claims that do not reproduce, and mixed definitions (1.13, 1.16, 1.17, 1.12, X11).** Germany's 7% → 2% in gensets (the 7% matches the UK), Germany in lasers 13% → 9% (flat), Thai forklifts defined two ways, and every Vietnam-based figure. Show HS codes and country sets, drop or fix the rest, and update Japan's Indonesian excavator share to its 2025 rebound (14%).
10. **Company-only anchors presented as fact (1.02–1.04, 1.15, 2.04, 2.21).** Komatsu's 29% → 20% and Hitachi's 21% → 17% are the companies' own estimates; the market-size figures are derived from them; "Most of what it lost went to Chinese brands" has no source; the "18-month lag" is the author's reading.
